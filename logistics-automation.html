The cost of manual work

Manual logistics work creates hidden operational costs

Many logistics teams rely on capable employees working across email, spreadsheets, documents, messaging apps, CRMs and legacy systems. The problem is not the team — it is the repetitive work between the systems.

Copying shipment details by hand

Data is read out of an email and typed into a spreadsheet or an internal system, one field at a time.

Preparing quotes manually

Every request is re-read, re-formatted and re-priced from scratch, even when the pattern repeats weekly.

Searching several systems for status

A single customer question sends someone through the mailbox, a carrier portal and a shared file.

Re-entering the same data

The same reference lands in a mailbox, a spreadsheet, a CRM and an operational platform — typed four times.

Processing PDFs and shipping documents

Invoices, packing lists and transport documents are opened, read and transcribed before anything moves.

Rebuilding the same reports

Daily and weekly summaries are assembled by hand from sources that have not changed in months.

Chasing customers and suppliers

Follow-ups depend on someone remembering, on the right day, across a long list of open files.

Sorting and routing requests

Incoming messages are triaged by a person before the responsible colleague ever sees them.

These tasks may look small individually, but together they consume hours every week and increase the risk of delays and errors.

Where automation pays off first

High-impact logistics workflows we can automate

The goal is not to remove human judgement. It is to remove the unnecessary repetitive work around it.

Example automation scenarios

The three scenarios below are written to explain how a workflow is restructured. They are illustrative examples, not client results, and no outcome is measured or guaranteed.

Example scenario

Freight quote intake

A freight-forwarding team receives quote requests by email in different formats.

Possible workflow

The system identifies the request, extracts origin, destination, dimensions, weight, service type and deadlines, then creates a structured task for the pricing team.

Potential impact

Faster intake, fewer missing fields and less manual copying.

Example scenario

Shipment update assistance

A customer-service team repeatedly checks shipment information and prepares similar status responses.

Possible workflow

The system gathers the available information, prepares a response draft, and flags exceptions for human review.

Potential impact

Shorter response times and more consistent communication.

Document processing

Possible workflow

Potential impact

Reduced data-entry workload and improved document visibility.

Questions

Frequently asked

If your question is not answered here, ask it directly in the audit request form below.

What logistics processes can be automated?

Processes involving repetitive emails, documents, data entry, reporting, follow-ups, routing and system updates are often good candidates. The exact opportunity depends on the workflow, available data, business rules and system access.

Do we need to replace our existing software?

Usually not. ArtX first evaluates whether useful automation can be added around the systems already in place. In some cases, technical limitations may require a different approach.

Can you work with legacy logistics systems?

Potentially. Feasibility depends on whether the system offers an API, database access, exports, email notifications, browser access or another reliable integration method.

Will AI send messages without human review?

Only if the agreed workflow allows it. Human approval can be required for quotes, sensitive customer communication, financial documents, exceptions and other high-impact actions.

How long does an automation project take?

A focused pilot may often take between two and six weeks. More complex projects can take longer depending on integrations, data quality, security requirements, testing and stakeholder availability.

How much does a project cost?

Pricing depends on workflow complexity, the number of integrations, document types, security requirements, support needs and implementation scope. The initial workflow audit helps define an appropriate pilot.

Can you automate WhatsApp communication?

Some WhatsApp workflows may be possible through approved WhatsApp Business solutions. Feasibility depends on the account setup, provider, consent requirements, message types and platform policies.

Is our company data secure?

Each project should be designed around the minimum required access, secure credential handling, appropriate permissions, testing controls and the client's security requirements. Specific security measures are agreed during project scoping.

Do you provide ongoing support?

Yes. Support, monitoring, maintenance and improvement options can be included based on the requirements of the implemented workflow.

Can you support multilingual logistics operations?

Yes. AI-assisted workflows may support multilingual classification, extraction, drafting and internal processing. Human review should remain available where accuracy or legal significance is important.

What happens during the free workflow audit?

We review one selected workflow, identify repetitive steps, assess potential automation opportunities, discuss system constraints and recommend a practical next step. The audit does not include full development or a complete technical specification.
