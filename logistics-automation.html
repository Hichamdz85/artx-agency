The cost of manual work

Manual logistics work creates hidden operational costs

Many logistics teams rely on capable employees working across email, spreadsheets, documents, messaging apps and legacy systems. The problem is not the team — it is the repetitive work between the systems.

Copying shipment details by hand

Data is read out of an email and typed into a spreadsheet or an internal system, one field at a time.

Preparing quotes manually

Every request is re-read, re-formatted and re-priced from scratch, even when the pattern repeats weekly.

Searching several systems for status

A single customer question sends someone through the mailbox, a carrier portal and a shared file.

Processing PDFs and shipping documents

Invoices, packing lists and transport documents are opened, read and transcribed before anything moves.

Rebuilding the same reports

Daily and weekly summaries are assembled by hand from sources that have not changed in months.

Sorting and routing requests

Incoming messages are triaged by a person before the responsible colleague ever sees them.

Questions

Frequently asked

If your question is not answered here, ask it directly in the audit request form below.

What logistics workflows can be automated?

Repetitive work involving email, documents, spreadsheets, quote requests, status updates and reporting is usually the best starting point. The exact opportunity depends on your workflow, the systems you use and the data available.

Do we need to replace our current software?

In most cases, no. We start by looking at how automation can work around the systems you already use, such as your email platform, CRM, ERP or spreadsheets. A different approach may be recommended if a system does not allow reliable access.

Can you work with legacy logistics systems?

Often, yes. Feasibility depends on whether the system offers an API, database access, file exports, email notifications or another reliable way to read and write data. This is assessed during the workflow audit.

How long does a pilot project take?

Timelines vary with integrations, data quality and security requirements, so we do not quote a fixed duration in advance. A pilot scope, with an estimated timeline, is defined together after the initial workflow audit.

How much does an automation project cost?

Pricing depends on the workflow, the number of systems involved, data quality and the security requirements of the project. We do not publish a fixed price, since a single figure would not reflect your actual scope. The workflow audit is used to size a pilot and give you a project-specific estimate.

How is our company data handled?

Each project is scoped around the minimum data and system access required, with attention to secure credential handling and role-based permissions. We take a GDPR-considerate approach for European clients, though final compliance responsibilities depend on your own systems, policies and legal obligations.

Will messages or decisions be sent without human review?

Not by default. Quotes, customer-facing messages, financial information and exceptions can be set to require employee approval before anything is sent or finalised. The review points are agreed with you during scoping.
